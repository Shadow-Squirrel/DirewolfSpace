Training and Exercises

Understand the threat. Then rehearse the decision.

Three courses and a tabletop exercise program for organizations whose operations, investments, or missions depend on space systems. Delivered on site at the customer's location, or virtually.

Curriculum

Three courses, each aimed at a different gap.

The courses draw on the same analytic approach behind Direwolf's intelligence products. They give staff and leadership a shared, accurate vocabulary for the threat environment, so that intelligence, when it arrives, is understood and acted on. Each course is scoped to the organization's mission and delivered on site or virtually.

Course 01

Space Threat Fundamentals

An introduction to threats to space systems and the missions that depend on them: counterspace weapons, RF interference, PNT disruption, and cyber threats to spacecraft and ground segments, and what each means for a mission.

Who it is for

Staff and leadership who need a shared, accurate picture of the threat environment before intelligence arrives.

Participants leave with

A working vocabulary for space threats, an understanding of how each class of threat affects a mission, and the ability to read an intelligence product on space threats and act on it.

Course 02

Foreign Space Capabilities

A survey of foreign space programs and capabilities and how to read their activity: what is on orbit, what it can do, and which activity is routine and which is not. Grounded in the same analytic approach behind Direwolf's assessments.

Who it is for

Staff and leadership whose mission requires an accurate reading of foreign space programs.

Participants leave with

An understanding of the foreign programs and capabilities relevant to their mission, and a disciplined way to read foreign space activity, so that routine activity is not mistaken for a threat and a threat is not dismissed as routine.

Course 03

Commercial Space Awareness

An introduction to the commercial space landscape for organizations that depend on it: the actors, the activity, the interference that affects commercial missions, and the exposure that comes with relying on commercial space services.

Who it is for

Operators and executives whose business depends on space, and the staff who support them.

Participants leave with

A clear picture of the commercial space environment, the risks to commercial missions, and the questions to ask before a decision that depends on space.

Tabletop exercises

You have a plan for a satellite anomaly or a GPS outage. Has anyone tested it?

A tabletop exercise places participants in a simulated space or cyber incident and requires them to decide, under time pressure and with incomplete information.

Direwolf delivers exercises at the scale the organization needs: a focused tabletop of two to four hours built around simulated space and cyber incidents and the decisions they force, or a multi-day exercise that incorporates orbital events, intelligence injects, cyberattacks, GPS disruption, jamming and spoofing, satellite anomalies, adversary activity, executive decision-making, and after-action reporting.

Every exercise is built on the organization's mission, its systems, and the decisions its people are responsible for. Direwolf runs it on site at the customer's location, or virtually.

Format 01

Focused tabletop

Two to four hours, built around simulated space and cyber incidents and the decisions they force. Suited to a leadership team, an operations floor, or a program office that needs to test one plan against one incident.

  • A scenario built on the organization's own systems and mission
  • Timed injects that change the picture as the session runs
  • A structured debrief of what was decided and what was missed
Format 02

Multi-day exercise

Runs over several days for organizations that need to exercise the whole decision chain, from operators to executives, against an evolving situation.

  • Orbital events, satellite anomalies, and adversary activity
  • Intelligence injects that change what participants know
  • Cyberattacks, GPS disruption, jamming and spoofing
  • Executive decision-making and formal after-action reporting

  1. Scenario

    A simulated space or cyber incident, built around the organization's mission and its systems.

  2. Injects

    New information arrives during the exercise and changes the picture: an orbital event, an anomaly, a signal, an intelligence report.

  3. Decisions

    Participants decide under time pressure and with incomplete information, as they would during a real incident.

  4. After-action

    The exercise closes with a structured review of what was decided, what was missed, and what to change. Multi-day exercises produce a formal written after-action report.
